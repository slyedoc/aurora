Water Medium Tracking

Every lake gets depth and colour: the water's medium is tracked along paths by its surface crossings in the main TLAS, not looked up in a capped table of boxes. Plus WoW's water types. A "lighter B": the camera is assumed above water. Plan, 2026-10-20.

Why. Today a water's medium is a WaterVolume box, and every water lookup (shadow rays, path segments, ReSTIR reconnections, the surface hit) loops over a table of boxes. That table is capped at the 64 nearest the camera (MAX_WATER_VOLUMES), so on the full WoW map (5,815 boxes) most lakes have no medium and render as clear glass. The surfaces are already in the main TLAS, and a path already hits them going in and out: track "in water" on the path, the way offline path tracers track media (PBRT medium interfaces, Cycles' volume stack), one level deep.

1. Decisions to review

D1. Scope: lighter B

  • Kept: per-path in-water state; absorption and the murk's scattering on in-water segments; underwater shadow/NEE (the sun reaching a lake bed); ReSTIR GI/PT segments through one helper; a leak guard.
  • Dropped for now: starting underwater (no camera probe: a path starts out of water); nested media (water in glass); WaterVolume boxes entirely.
  • Later, small and separate: a per-frame camera-underwater probe (one ray up against water surfaces, Cycles' "volume stack init") turns diving on.

D2. Where a water's material lives: on the surface

WaterSurface requires WaterMaterial3d (moved from WaterVolume). Its row in a GPU water-material table rides the existing per-material surface-class row (SurfaceGroupData::set_row, read in shaders as the material record's surface_param_index): the water class's row is the medium index. The shadow any-hit also needs to know a hit is water: a flag bit in the material record (see M3).

D3. WoW water types: a WaterKind preset component

WaterKind { Lake, River, Ocean, Pond, Swamp } on a surface (reflected, an enum jackdaw edits) inserts the matching shared preset WaterMaterial3d (aurora keeps one asset per kind, so a kind shares one FFT wave job). Presets: CLEAR_LAKE, RIVER (new: clear-green, shorter waves), OCEAN (new: deeper blue-green, long waves), MURKY_POND, SWAMP.

D4. One crossing per straight segment

A shadow ray or reconnection segment is assumed to cross the water surface at most once: its any-hit keeps the nearest water crossing. Paths crossing twice in one straight segment (in and out again through a wave) are rare and get the first crossing only.

D5. Refraction stays approximated for shadows

As now: a shadow ray passes the surface straight, takes the Fresnel transmittance and the underwater length along the refracted direction for the same depth. No caustics from shadow rays (offline engines do the same).

2. Types and interfaces

// aurora src/water_volume.rs (rename the module to water.rs if you like; keep the plugin name)
#[derive(Component, Reflect, Clone, Copy, Debug, Default, PartialEq, Eq)]
#[reflect(Component, Default)]
#[require(WaterSurface)]
pub enum WaterKind { #[default] Lake, River, Ocean, Pond, Swamp }

#[derive(Component, Reflect, Clone, Copy, Debug, Default)]
#[reflect(Component, Default)]
#[require(WaterMaterial3d)]
pub struct WaterSurface;          // was a bare marker; now carries the medium via WaterMaterial3d

// WaterMaterial gains: pub const RIVER, pub const OCEAN
// Resource WaterPresets: one Handle<WaterMaterial> per WaterKind (Assets::add at finish()).
// A WaterKind added/changed -> insert WaterMaterial3d(presets[kind]).
// WaterVolume, GpuWaterVolume's transform rows, WaterVolumeTable's per-volume rows: removed.

// GPU: a water-material table (one row per WaterMaterial in use), replacing the volume table:
struct GpuWaterMedium { absorption: [f32;3], ior: f32, scattering: [f32;3], anisotropy: f32,
                        wave_direction: [f32;2], wavelength: f32, steepness: f32, wave_speed: f32,
                        wave_tex: [u32;2], wave_tile: f32 }   // = GpuWaterVolume less `rows`

The uniform keeps two fields under new names: water_volumes -> the medium table's address, water_volume_count -> its row count (same offsets: the layout tests in ray_render_plugin.rs pin them, and water_surface.slang reads them as float indices 98 and 187).

4. Steps (each builds and runs on its own)

  1. M1 Material on the surface. WaterSurface requires WaterMaterial3d; WaterKind, WaterPresets, RIVER/OCEAN consts; the medium table (one row per material in use) replaces the volume table; the water class's SurfaceGroupData::set_row per surface material gives each surface its row. water_surface.slang reads its row instead of searching boxes. Check: the pond example's surface looks as before (waves, Fresnel), still with its old box for the medium.
  2. M2 Path state. The in-water state in the path loop (set on refraction down through a water-class hit, cleared on refraction up); in-water segments take absorption + free-flight scattering from that row; the leak guard (in water, hitting a water surface from above: resync to that surface). waterSegment becomes a one-medium function. Check: the pond's depth tiles.
  3. M3 Shadow crossings. ShadowPayload grows water_t and water_row (+ facing); the material record gets a water flag bit, set for water-class materials; shadow_any_hit keeps the nearest water hit before ignoring it (transmissive surfaces already pass). waterAlong replaces waterLightTransmittance at every call site. Check: the sunlit lake bed in the pond, against a reference.
  4. M4 ReSTIR. PT replay tracks state from its re-traced hits; reconnection and GI segments use waterAlong. Check: ReSTIR GI on vs off, unbiased under water and from the bank.
  5. M5 Boxes out, WoW in. Remove WaterVolume and its code; the pond example keeps just its surface (WaterKind::Lake or the inspector's material). aurora_files' WoW importer: drop water_boxes and terrain_cell_floor; emit one surface per liquid kind per tile (ocean / lake / river cells), each with its WaterKind; magma as the lava surface. Rebake the full map (wow_import --all ... --out-dir zero/assets/wow_ek) and, if wanted, Northshire.
